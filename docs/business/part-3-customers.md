## What this tells us (first reading)

- **Customers:** private companies and oil companies; sometimes government.
- **Agreements are verbal.** Job length unknown until the customer says stop, unless a contract states it.
- **Customer pricing today: per trip or per ton (MT).** Must vary by job, by route and by quantity.
- **Prices change during a job.** Example: the diesel-to-Syria job is billed to the customer at **120 USD/MT** (driver gets 70 USD/MT), but the customer price can change after some days as transport costs change, and the set of fees included in the price can change too (a fee included in the old price may not be in the new one).
  → Customer price and "what the price includes" must be **dated within a job**: each trip takes the price and the included fees in force on its date (loading date — to confirm).
- **Payment:** cash. Job in IQD → paid in IQD; job in USD → paid in USD. **Sometimes the balance is settled with product** (barter: the customer gives product instead of money).
- **Customer deductions:** the customer applies its own allowance ("forgiveness") and shortage fines to us, at its own price per MT. Their allowance can be **per truck or per MT** (ours to drivers is per trip in tons). → Two separate shortage calculations per trip: customer → us (on the invoice) and us → driver (on the settlement). The difference is our gain or loss on shortages.
- **Late payment** is normal; nothing can be done, but the money comes eventually. → Need clear receivable tracking: what each customer owes, per job and per invoice, how old it is, and their advances to us.
